Sidenote privacy policy

Updated November 7, 2026 · covers version 1.7 and AI access

Your notes live in your own browser. Nothing leaves it unless you turn on sync or share a notepad, and even then your notes are encrypted on your device first, so the sync service cannot read them. If you choose to connect an AI assistant to your notes, the section on AI assistants below explains exactly what changes. Sidenote has no accounts, no analytics and no ads, and sells or shares nothing.

What Sidenote stores

Sidenote is a Chrome extension for writing notes in the side panel. It stores these things, all on your device:

  • Your notepads and settings, in chrome.storage.local. This is local browser storage. Chrome does not sync it to your Google account or anywhere else.
  • Images you add to notes, in the extension's IndexedDB storage, after Sidenote shrinks them on your computer. Chrome does not sync this either.
  • Which notepad each tab is showing, in chrome.storage.session. Chrome clears this when the browser closes.

With sync and sharing off (the default), Sidenote makes no network requests of its own, with one exception you choose: a YouTube video or a Google Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides or Forms file you put in a note loads from Google while the note is open, as it would on any web page, under Google's privacy policy. YouTube videos load through a small page on sidenote-sync.netlify.app, because YouTube won't play inside an extension otherwise. The video's address goes in the part of that page's address after “#”, which browsers never send, so the page learns nothing about what you watch. Sidenote has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code.

Sync and sharing (optional)

You can turn on sync in the full view's settings, under Sync & devices, or share a single notepad with Copy share key in its menu. Both work with keys instead of accounts:

  • A sync key links your own computers. A share key gives someone access to one notepad and its subpads: an edit key lets them change it, a view-only key lets them read it but never change it. Keys are made at random on your device. Anyone who has a key can read and edit what it covers, so treat keys like passwords.
  • What is sent. The notepads covered by your keys (title, text, colour and place in your list) and, with sync on, a small index of which notepads you have, which you starred or archived, their order and your look settings. All of it is encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM, using a key derived from your Sidenote key, before it is sent. Images in those notepads go up once each, encrypted the same way with a key of their own that is written inside the note, so whoever can read the note can see its images.
  • What the service sees. The sync service at sidenote-sync.netlify.app, run by the developer on Netlify, stores the encrypted data under an identifier that is a one-way hash of your key, with a revision number and a time. Your keys themselves never leave your device, so the service cannot decrypt anything. Like any website, Netlify keeps standard request logs, which include IP addresses, under Netlify's privacy policy.
  • What it is used for. Only to keep your notepads in step between your devices and the people you shared them with. It is never read, analysed, sold or used for anything else.
  • Deleting. Deleting a notepad you made replaces its stored copy with an encrypted deletion marker on the next sync, which removes it from every device that has it. Removing a notepad someone shared with you only removes your copy. Turning sync off on a device stops it syncing and deletes nothing. An image you remove from a note stays on the service, encrypted; without a note that holds its key it can't be found or read.

AI assistants (optional)

You can let an AI assistant such as Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini read and edit your synced notepads, using your sync key. It is off unless you set it up, and there are several ways to do it:

  • On your computer (the local server). Some apps (Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Gemini CLI, Codex and others) can run the open-source Sidenote server on your own computer. Your sync key is saved in that app's settings or your system keychain, your notes are decrypted on your computer, and the sync service still only ever sees encrypted data.
  • The hosted connector at sidenote-sync.netlify.app/mcp, for apps that connect by web address (ChatGPT, claude.ai, the Gemini app). When you connect one, you type your sync key into Sidenote's own sign-in page, not into the AI app. The key is then sealed with AES-256-GCM, under a secret only the connector holds, into the sign-in tokens the AI app keeps, so the AI app never sees your key. Each time the assistant uses your notes, the connector opens the token, decrypts the notepads it needs in memory, does what was asked, returns the result, and forgets it. This is the one case where your notes are readable on the server, and only while a request is being answered. The connector stores no keys and no notes. It keeps only a one-way hash of your key with a time if you use Disconnect (below), and a marker that each sign-in code has been used once.
  • Sidenote on the web at sidenote-sync.netlify.app/notes. You open your notes there with your sync key, or a shared notepad with its key, and they are decrypted in your browser tab, like in the extension. Edits are encrypted there before they are saved. The key is kept in the page's address after “#”, a part browsers never send to a server, so you can bookmark the page; that also puts the key in your browser history and bookmarks, so anyone who can use your browser, or who gets the address, can open those notes. The page remembers which notepad you had open (in this browser only), never the key itself.
  • Links for chat apps. If you choose “Link for chat apps” on that page, or “Copy a link for chat apps” in Sidenote’s settings under Sync & devices, your sync key is sent to the connector once and sealed with AES-256-GCM, under a secret only the connector holds, into a read-only link. The key itself never appears in the link. When a chat app opens the link, the connector decrypts your notepads in memory, returns them as text and forgets them. It stores no keys and no notes. Anyone who has the link can read your notepads (not change them) until you turn links off (below). Links ask search engines not to index them.
  • The sidenote command-line tool (sidenote.mjs), for assistants that can run commands on your computer. It works like the local server: your key is kept on your computer (in your user folder if you save it), and notes are decrypted there.
  • What the AI app receives. Whatever the assistant reads through these tools (titles and text of the notepads it opens or searches, and images it asks to see) goes to that AI provider, such as OpenAI, Anthropic or Google, and is handled under its privacy policy. Changes the assistant makes are written to your notepads like any other edit. When an assistant adds an image from a web address, the connector or the command-line tool downloads it from that address and stores it encrypted like any other image.
  • How long access lasts. A connection's access token lasts an hour and is renewed automatically for up to 180 days. To turn off every link you made and sign every AI app out, use Turn off AI access: links stop at once, apps within an hour. You can also remove Sidenote in the AI app's settings. Choose “Read only” when connecting if the assistant should never change anything.
  • The connector's code is public at github.com/ElMagoCT/sidenote-mcp. Netlify keeps standard request logs (including IP addresses) for it, as for the sync service.

Permissions and why they are needed

  • Side panel shows your notepad in Chrome's side panel, only on the tab where you open it.
  • Storage saves your notepads and settings in your browser, as described above.
  • Tabs is used in two cases. When you choose "Link this page", Sidenote reads the current tab's address and title and puts a link to it in your note. When you open the full view, Sidenote looks for an existing Sidenote tab so it can switch to it instead of opening a second one. Sidenote does not record or keep your browsing history.
  • Alarms schedules the reminders you write in a note (for example “@tomorrow 9am”), on your computer.
  • Notifications are optional. Sidenote asks the first time you make a reminder, then shows it at the time you set. Nothing about a reminder is sent anywhere beyond your synced notes.
  • Unlimited storage lifts Chrome's 10 MB limit on local storage so a large set of notes keeps saving. It does not send anything anywhere.
  • Scripting and access to web pages are optional and off by default. If you turn on the page shortcut in Settings, Chrome asks for permission to run a small script on web pages. It only watches for the key combination you chose and tells Sidenote to open; it does not read or send anything from the page. Turning it off removes the permission.

Links you open

When you click a web link inside a note, Chrome opens that site in a new tab. From then on, that site's own privacy policy applies. Sidenote is not involved in the visit.

Your control

  • Export all notepads as a JSON file from the settings sheet in the full view. The file is saved to your computer.
  • Delete any notepad from its menu.
  • Turn sync off on any device from Settings → Sync & devices.
  • Disconnect AI assistants at sidenote-sync.netlify.app/oauth/disconnect, or remove Sidenote from the AI app.
  • Uninstall the extension and Chrome deletes everything Sidenote stored.

Children

Sidenote collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. Version 1.2 added the optional, encrypted sync and sharing described above. Version 1.5 added view-only share keys and reminders (alarms, and notifications if you allow them). Sync keeps no extra copy of your notes on your computer, only small fingerprints of each line, used to merge edits made at the same time. November 2026 added the optional AI assistant connections described above, then the Sidenote for AI page, read-only links for chat apps, the command-line tool and Sidenote on the web. Version 1.7 added images (shrunk on your computer, encrypted like notes when synced), YouTube and Google file embeds, and whiteboards.

Contact
